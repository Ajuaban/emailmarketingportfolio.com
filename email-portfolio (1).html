Deliverability

A new sending domain, set up in the right order.

Good copy only works if the message arrives. This is the order I would work in for a brand-new domain.

The short version

For a brand-new sending domain, I'd authenticate a dedicated subdomain with SPF, DKIM, and DMARC (starting at p=none with reporting, then tightening once the reports are clean), then warm it up by sending small batches to the most engaged, opted-in subscribers and scaling only while bounces and complaints stay low.

  1. Choose the sending domain

    Use a subdomain such as mail.brand.com, so email reputation stays separate from the main website and everyday business mail.

  2. Publish SPF

    Add the platform's SPF include to DNS. Keep a single SPF record and stay inside the ten-lookup limit.

  3. Sign with DKIM

    Add the platform's DKIM records so each message is signed by the domain and can be verified by the mailbox provider.

  4. Publish DMARC

    Start at p=none with a reporting address. Read the reports, fix any legitimate sender that fails, then move to quarantine and reject.

  5. Warm up gradually

    Begin with the most engaged contacts who clearly opted in, in small batches. Raise volume only while bounces and complaints stay low.

  6. Monitor every send

    Watch bounces, complaints, engagement, and blocklists. Use Google Postmaster Tools once the volume is high enough to show data.

Consent and unsubscribe

  • Express opt-in, with a record. When, where, and what the person agreed to.
  • Clear sender identity. An accurate From name, honest subject lines, and a mailing address in every email.
  • A working unsubscribe in every email. Honoured promptly. CASL and CAN-SPAM both allow up to 10 business days, so I aim for immediately.
  • A clean suppression list. Unsubscribes and hard bounces are never re-imported.

How I work in a restricted niche

  • Start from the client's approved claims. If a line isn't on the list, it doesn't go in.
  • Read the platform's acceptable-use policy first. Some email platforms restrict certain categories, so I confirm before building anything.
  • Keep everything portable. Templates, flows, and DNS records are documented so a program can move quickly if an account is closed.

This is how I work, not legal advice. Final claims should come from the client's approved list and their own counsel.
